DIY vs. Professional

Roll up your sleeves, or pick up the phone?

A general guide. Skill levels vary, and many projects require permits in Michigan, so check with your local building department before you start.

Often DIY-friendly

  • Interior painting and touch-ups
  • Re-caulking tubs, sinks, and windows
  • Swapping cabinet hardware and faucet aerators
  • Replacing furnace filters and smoke-alarm batteries
  • Installing peel-and-stick backsplash or shelving

Usually call a licensed pro

  • Electrical panels, new circuits, and rewiring
  • Gas lines and furnace or water-heater installs
  • Removing or altering walls that may be load-bearing
  • Roofing and major plumbing changes
  • Anything involving suspected asbestos or lead paint

Home Maintenance

A Michigan homeowner's year

Full checklist (coming soon) →

Spring

Check the sump pump, clear gutters and downspouts, and look for winter damage on the roof and siding.

Summer

Service the air conditioner, reseal decks, and check grading so water drains away from the foundation.

Fall

Schedule a furnace tune-up, shut off and drain outdoor faucets, and clear leaves before the first freeze.

Winter

Change furnace filters, watch for ice dams, and test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.

Value-Minded Upgrades

Upgrades that may help your home's value

No project guarantees a return. Value depends on your home, your neighborhood, and the market when you sell, so treat these as places to start the conversation.

  • Curb appealFront door, lighting, landscaping, and a tidy entry.
  • Energy efficiencyInsulation, sealing, and efficient heating, which matter in Michigan winters.
  • Kitchen refreshPaint, hardware, lighting, and counters before a full gut.
  • Deferred maintenanceA sound roof, dry basement, and working systems come first.
