Understanding Home Equity: What It Is and How It Grows

Equity is one of the most talked-about parts of owning a home, and one of the least explained. Here's how to estimate yours, what builds it, and what to weigh before borrowing against it.

By Johnice Edwards · October 9, 2026 · 7 min read

What is home equity?

Home equity is the part of your home you truly own. It's the difference between what your home is worth today and what you still owe on it, including your mortgage and any other loans secured by the property.

A simple example

Estimated value $250,000 − loan balance $180,000 = $70,000 in equity

Hypothetical numbers for illustration only.

Your home's value is always an estimate until it sells. Online estimates, a comparative market analysis, or a professional appraisal can each give you a different number, so treat equity as a range rather than a fixed amount.

How equity grows

  • Your down payment. Equity starts on day one with the portion of the price you paid up front.
  • Paying down principal. Each mortgage payment includes some principal. Early on, most of a payment goes to interest. Over time, more goes toward the balance.
  • Market appreciation. If home values in your area rise, so can your equity. Values can also fall, which is why equity isn't guaranteed.
  • Improvements and upkeep. Some projects may increase value. Keeping up with maintenance helps protect the value you already have.

Ways homeowners access equity

Homeowners sometimes borrow against equity for renovations, education, or consolidating debt. Common options include:

  • Home equity loan: a lump sum, usually at a fixed rate, repaid over a set term.
  • Home equity line of credit (HELOC): a revolving line you draw from as needed, often with a variable rate.
  • Cash-out refinance: replacing your mortgage with a larger one and taking the difference in cash.

Before you borrow

Each of these options uses your home as collateral. If you can't repay, you could risk losing your home. Compare costs and fees, and talk with a qualified lender or financial professional about your situation.

The takeaway

Equity is a long-term outcome of steady payments, careful upkeep, and time. Knowing roughly where you stand helps you plan renovations, think about refinancing, and make calmer decisions if you ever decide to sell.
